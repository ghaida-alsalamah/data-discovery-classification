# What the benchmark measures

The previous benchmark labels were reused as written. Their broad personal designation can include a transaction, service event, or internal reference associated with a person. A span recognizer can detect an email or phone number in those tables, while it needs separate relational evidence to determine whether the transaction or service event concerns an identifiable individual. The fresh fixtures explicitly label direct, linked, non-personal, and uncertain cases.

The original engine's exact semantics and field domains are separate outputs. For example, on the development fixtures it correctly classified `orders.amount` as a transaction amount and missed its association with a buyer despite a confidence of 0.908. It detected `fieldStaff.service_email` exactly and assigned the catalog domain instead of workforce at 0.96. These are genuine contextual errors. The benchmark's strict domain match also penalizes distinctions outside the engine taxonomy, such as municipal services. On the fresh fixtures, strict domain accuracy was 29.2% and accuracy with out-of-taxonomy domains treated as unknown was 58.3%. Both use all 72 fields. The open-set variant changes the expected label for unsupported domains to unknown.

OpenMetadata's catalog bundles capabilities relevant to organization-wide discovery, governance and profiling. Its ingestion auto-classifier uses sensitive column-name recognition and value sampling with configurable language and tagging thresholds. The released standalone classifier exposes native PII types based on heuristics and Presidio. It would overlap with the existing detection layer and still require the Saudi and relational layers for this project. The catalog also requires a running service and connector credentials. The current scan runs directly against SQLite. The auto-classification ingestion package carries the Collate Community License, separately from the repository root license. [OpenMetadata auto-classification](https://docs.open-metadata.org/v1.12.x/how-to-guides/data-governance/classification/auto-classification) · [Ingestion license](https://github.com/open-metadata/OpenMetadata/blob/main/ingestion/LICENSE)

DoDuo uses a joint serialization of table columns with the published BERT checkpoint. Its VizNet taxonomy contains web-table types such as `city`, `currency`, and `name`. A generic `name` can describe a product, organization, or person. Saudi ID subtypes and the project’s business domains do not appear as native labels. The scanner exports source labels and scores, maps only predefined compatible labels, and exposes mapped labels as review suggestions when the current engine abstains. [DoDuo source and pretrained checkpoints](https://github.com/megagonlabs/doduo)

Sato is also a contextual VizNet classifier and uses the same 78-type family. DoDuo was prioritized for execution because its released implementation directly accepts dataframe tables. ArcheType uses task-specific inputs and label descriptions with a language model. It was reviewed as an alternative for expanding semantic coverage. No Sato or ArcheType performance is inferred from the other models' results. [Sato](https://github.com/megagonlabs/sato) · [ArcheType](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18208)

The scanner maintains the project’s six observable capability areas. SQLite connection and asset access remain read-only. Schema and relationships are exported under `schema` and `relationships`. Field shape and quality are exported under `profiles` and `quality`. Semantic, sensitivity, direct PII, broader personal-data status, and review details are under `classifications`. Field-level domain assignments roll up under `domains`, and analysis readiness is under `opportunities`. The original attachments specify these capabilities across Phases 1–3. They do not provide the verbatim Elm six-item rubric.
